# fueledbychai-rs

Venue-agnostic trading connectivity for Rust: market data and order entry behind common traits,
with one crate per venue. The Rust counterpart of
[FueledByChaiTrading](https://github.com/FueledByChai/FueledByChaiTrading).

First venues: Paradex, then Hibachi; Binance USD-M futures for reference market data.

Status: scaffolding. The Cargo workspace exists with ten crates:

| Crate | Status |
| --- | --- |
| `fbc-core` | Time kinds, units, exact prices (`PxExact`), money and price grids (fixed, significant-figure, banded); sealed order and fill ids, the canonical client-id codec, restart-safe minting under a namespace lease, `Fee` (positive means we paid) and the per-account `FeeBook`, `DecodeScope`, `InstrumentSpec` with maker-safe quantization, `VenueCaps` with every field mandatory (order and fill capabilities together in one optional `exec` block, `None` for market data only; decision 0015), and the venue boundary: market-data and execution events, venue commands and their outcomes, and the sans-IO `MdCodec`, `ExecCodec`, `OrderSigner`, `NonceSource` and `VenueFactory` traits with `Effects` and `EncodeCtx` |
| `fbc-book` | A tick-indexed L2 book per instrument and book channel, built from normalized book events the same live and in replay: snapshots replace it, deltas set and remove levels, a window bounds it, a gap invalidates it until the next complete snapshot; touch, top-n, one level's size where the book knows it (inside its window, or no deeper than the deepest level a capped book shows), an exact top-n comparison naming the first differing level, and canonical bytes |
| `fbc-oms` | Order truth, its first slice (decision 0005): the order record, whose ranked states (PendingNew and Unknown, Open, PartiallyFilled, Terminal) only move forward with a terminal state absorbing; venue order updates applied under the venue's ordering key with an ingest tiebreak, a terminal update always applying unless it names a superseded venue id, an amend's new venue id superseding the old; each command item's outcome (a placement not sent or refused ends the order, an accepted one opens it, an unanswered one is Unknown, fully resting and never resent, a refused amend or cancel leaves the order alive); and the registry by client id; the fill ledger, deduplicating fills by their key within an age and a count the consumer configures, applying a replayed fill only when absent and newer than both the session-start watermark and the ledger's retention horizon, and handing each accepted fill once to the registry, the one path that moves an order's fill sum and the inventory, with filled quantity the larger of the venue's cumulative count and that sum, never their sum. I1 (for order updates and with fills), I2 and I3 are property-tested with proptest under fixed seeds (decision 0037). The gateway traits live here: a gateway takes an order-affecting command only as an `Authorization` that only `fbc-oms` issues, carrying its market's state generation, never cloned and spent on submit (decision 0045). The execution planner diffs the consumer's desired book per side and level against the orders it placed and emits authorized cancels, then reducing orders, then amends, then adds, amending only where the venue's order capabilities allow and otherwise cancelling and placing once the old order is terminal (decision 0065) |
| `fbc-sim` | The queue-position fill model (BT-501), mechanism only: each simulated resting order's place in its level's queue under a Pessimistic, Middle or Optimistic bracket chosen by the caller (no default), queued on arrival behind the public book's size at its price less every modelled public order, and only where the book knows that size (an RPI order behind every public order at its price, public size that joins later included), advanced by a level cancel by none, the floor of the proportional share or all of the cancelled size, and filled by a trade at its price only once the queue ahead is consumed, or by a trade through its price, which empties the queue ahead, each trade's size spent once in match order (decision 0038); and SimVenue, a simulated venue whose codec is an unchanged `ExecCodec` writing places and cancels as frames on a simulated order-entry stream, and whose engine, a pure state machine fed the shard's envelopes and those frames, crosses orders against the displayed book, rejects a crossing post-only order, fills resting orders through the queue model, and answers after the consumer's latency with fills charged from the consumer's fee book (decision 0046), and answers a resync with its resting orders and the positions its fills imply (decision 0049), ordering its answers by the stood-in venue's key, its fills carrying the realized P&L and funding the venue declares and a derived-fills venue's fills sent as order updates (decision 0050); no calibration, which is the consumer's |
| `fbc-journal` | The journal's record format (inbound frames with their stamps, outbound frames and write results, HTTP requests and results with headers, timers, control records and markers), a writer of one subdirectory per UTC day with `<shard>-<seq>` segments rolled every UTC hour, each closed segment compressed with zstd, and a reader in write order of the compressed and open segments; redaction spans and secret headers are never written, each stored as its HMAC-SHA-256 under a key the consumer supplies, with signatures kept verbatim; a sink that never blocks, with a soft limit for normal records, a safety reserve, drops counted by class and a `Degraded` marker, drained by a writer thread (decisions 0006, 0021, 0024, 0026) |
| `fbc-runtime` | The one connector every connection goes through (`ProxyConfig::Direct` or `ProxyConfig::Socks5 { host, port }`, a hand-written no-authentication SOCKS5 CONNECT that names the target by host name so the proxy resolves it), with a WebSocket client (`ws://`, `wss://`) and an HTTP/1.1 call (`http://`, `https://`) over it; TLS runs on the connector's stream with rustls and the ring provider, trusting webpki-roots plus anchors the consumer adds (decisions 0019, 0020); errors name the failed step and never the URL. Connection epochs that drop and count a dead connection's late frames, timers, HTTP results and events, and a subscription reconciler that sends only the difference and each epoch's set once, as logic without a socket. A market-data session (`MdSession`) that drives one socket endpoint over them: a fresh codec per epoch, events stamped and handed to the consumer's `MdHandler` in ingest order, the codec's frames, timers and reconnects executed, and reconnects paced by the consumer's backoff, attempt budget and attempt deadline (decision 0023); the codec's keepalive sent at its interval, the connection rotated to its next epoch the consumer's margin before the venue's `max_conn_lifetime`, and a stream silent for the consumer's window reported stale and reconnected (decision 0033); a write a peer stopped reading abandoned after the consumer's write-stall window, ending the epoch as a drop, with the timers that fall due meanwhile fired at their times (decision 0036); the HTTP requests a codec asks for, run with their timeouts and answered only to the epoch that asked, and poll endpoints that open no connection; and a venue's endpoints (`MdVenue`) opened, kept and closed as `plan_md` spreads the desired subscriptions over them (decision 0027); and the books (`MdBooks`, fed by the `BookKeeper` handler): one `fbc-book` book per instrument and book channel, each book event routed by its `BookId`, and each instrument's configured trading book exposed; with a `Journal` set, every session records what crosses the shard boundary into the consumer's `fbc-journal` sink (inbound frames with their stamps before decoding, outbound frames and write results, HTTP requests and results, stale ones included, timer firings, connections opened and closed, subscribe calls), each under its traffic class, and never waits on it: a record with no room is dropped and counted, and a Safety frame is written all the same (decision 0006); and rate-limit buckets per declared limit and scope key (account, IP, instrument, connection), sliding windows charged by each frame's, HTTP request's and connection attempt's rate charge, with normal traffic stopping at a consumer-configured safety reserve, refusals and 429/418 responses counted by scope, a refused subscription sent when its bucket refills and a refused connection attempt waiting for it (decision 0030); and, on Linux, each frame stamped with the kernel receive time of its last packet (`SO_TIMESTAMPNS`, read beneath TLS and WebSocket) and each Safety-class write issued while a frame is handled, by the codec or through the handler's `Outbox`, reported to the handler as its tick-to-wire (decision 0031; `None` and nothing reported elsewhere) |
| `fbc-venue-binance-usdm` | Binance USD-M futures, market data only (the reference feed, never traded; decisions 0015, 0016): capabilities with `exec: None` and the documented rate limits (request weight per IP, messages per connection, new connections per IP), configuration for the base URLs, the partial-depth stream and the diff-depth snapshot, subscriptions planned on the `/public` combined-stream endpoint, and a codec that subscribes live and decodes `bookTicker` into touches, partial depth into complete book snapshots, and the diff-depth book anchored on a REST snapshot with its gaps, duplicates and out-of-order events detected and resynced |
| `fbc-conformance` | A public stub venue server for tests, on 127.0.0.1 ephemeral ports: a WebSocket endpoint that plays a fault script of typed steps (accept, read, push, close, go silent, each naming its connection) and records every connection and data frame, and an HTTP/1.1 endpoint with fixed responses by path; the 340-reconnect storm script, and a check of the attempts a stub saw against the client's reconnect pacing (decision 0025); the conformance toy venue (`src/toy/`, decision 0044), so far its order entry: every command kind encoded and signed through `ExecCodec`, each declared order capability checked before signing, its order updates, fills, rejects and venue modes decoded through `DecodeScope`, and its answers: queries, batch items answered in separate frames, resyncs and authentication |
| `fbc-venue-paradex` | The Paradex signer (`src/sign`, owner-reviewed): the SNIP-12 revision 0 typed-data hash of `Order`, `ModifyOrder` and the auth `Request` and its Stark-curve ECDSA signature with RFC 6979 nonces, behind `OrderSigner`, held to the Java library's vectors in `fixtures/paradex/signing/`. Market data (`src/md`): an SBE reader gated on each frame's stated block lengths, `bbo` and `trades` decoded into touches and trades, the `order_book` deltas channels into book events with every seq_no discontinuity reported and resynced by reconnect (0022), the REST `/orderbook` snapshot at depth 15 with offline book and bbo-touch agreement checks against the delta-built book (`tests/oracle/`), a decoder-replay test (`tests/replay.rs`) that runs the codec through a session against the conformance stub and rebuilds two markets' books byte-identically from the session's journal (0006), authentication (`src/auth`, owner-reviewed): the `/auth` login signed from `Secrets` with its signature expiry, the session token named for the journal and sent only redacted, its refresh on a configured timer, and Test Connection as a two-round plan (0048), order entry's capabilities (`src/exec`: `ExecCaps` with every value cited, the order rate limits, and the order socket's SBE schema 1:2, 0054), the REST resync of open orders and positions and the order query by client id from `/orders-history`, decoded whole into the resync events and a `QueryResult` (`src/exec/rest.rs`), the read-only private-stream codec, which logs in, authenticates the socket with the token in a redaction span, subscribes the orders, fills, positions and account channels once per connection, decodes them and refuses every command (`src/exec/read_only.rs`, 0061), the order-entry codec `ParadexExec` on the same connection (`src/exec/codec.rs`, 0071), and a `VenueFactory` declaring market data and order entry: one order-entry endpoint on SBE 1:2 and, as the required `paradex.exec.mode` says, the order-entry or the read-only codec built from `Secrets` through `src/auth` (0072; the snapshot source stays untrustworthy, so no live Paradex session yet), held to hand-built frames in `fixtures/paradex/md/` and responses in `fixtures/paradex/rest/` |
| `fbc-examples` | Owner-run sample programs, built by CI but never run against a venue: `md_watch` subscribes to a Paradex market (bbo, trades, `deltas` book) and a Binance USD-M symbol (`bookTicker`, diff-depth book) through `fbc-runtime`, keeps one book per channel, and prints a line per touch, book change, trade, feed health change and reconnect; it never sends an order or reads a credential, and its wiring is tested against the conformance stub |

No venue is supported yet: Paradex has its signer, its authentication, its bbo, trades and order book market data, and order entry wired into its factory, but no live session until a decision record trusts its resync on testnet evidence (0054, 0072); Binance USD-M
decodes its touch, partial depth and diff-depth book; `MdSession` makes the diff-depth book's REST snapshot request (the
codec's `Effect::Http`, decision 0027), and `tests/replay.rs` runs that codec through a session against the
conformance stub and rebuilds its books byte-identically by decoder replay of the session's journal (0006).

To watch live market data (public channels only, no credentials):

```
cargo run -p fbc-examples --example md_watch -- --paradex BTC-USD-PERP --binance BTCUSDT
cargo run -p fbc-examples --example md_watch -- --help
```
